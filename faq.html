Sizing & Selection

Finding the right BTU for your space.

A general rule: 20 BTU per square foot. Use these as a starting guide:

• 9,000 BTU — up to ~450 sq ft (bedroom, office)
• 12,000 BTU — up to ~600 sq ft (medium room, studio)
• 18,000 BTU — up to ~900 sq ft (large room, open plan)
• 24,000 BTU — up to ~1,200 sq ft (large open area)
• 36,000 BTU — up to ~1,800 sq ft (large home, workshop)

Factors like high ceilings, lots of windows, or poor insulation may require sizing up. When in doubt, contact us or use our BTU calculator.
Always size up rather than down. An undersized unit will run constantly, wear out faster, and never fully cool your space. An oversized unit costs a bit more upfront but handles your needs comfortably and lasts longer. If you’re between two sizes, go with the larger one.
SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) measures how efficiently a unit cools. A higher SEER means lower electricity bills. All of our units carry a 17–20 SEER2 rating, which exceeds federal minimum efficiency standards and will save you money on energy costs year over year.
Yes — all of our units are heat pumps that operate in both heating and cooling modes. The Advantage 5th Gen models are rated for heating down to approximately 14°F — suitable for most temperate climates across the US. In mild climates, a mini split can replace your furnace entirely. Switching between modes is as simple as pressing a button on the remote or the app.
A single-zone system has one outdoor unit connected to one indoor unit — it cools or heats one room or area. A multi-zone system has one outdoor unit connected to two or more indoor units, letting you control different rooms independently.

All units sold on this site are single-zone. For whole-home coverage, multiple single-zone units or a multi-zone system may be the right fit. Contact us if you need help planning a larger installation.

Installation

DIY setup & what’s included.

Yes — that’s one of the main advantages of this product line. These are designed for DIY installation and do not require an HVAC license in most states. The kit includes the indoor unit, outdoor unit, lineset, electrical disconnect, and all hardware needed to complete the install. Most installations take 4–8 hours with basic tools.
Standard installation requires: a drill with hole saw bit, level, adjustable wrench, wire strippers, and a voltage meter. No vacuum pump or refrigerant certification is required because the line sets come pre-charged — you just connect them.
Standard 230V units require a dedicated 240V circuit. If you don’t already have one, you’ll need a licensed electrician to install the breaker and run the wiring to the outdoor disconnect. The rest of the installation you can do yourself.

115V Plug-In units (our 9k and 12k plug-in models) do not require any new electrical work. If you already have a standard 115V/20A outlet near where the outdoor unit will go, no electrician is needed at all.
Requirements depend on the model:

Standard 230V units run on a dedicated 208–230V / 60Hz circuit. Breaker size depends on the BTU: smaller units typically need a 15–20 amp breaker, while larger units (24k–36k) may require a 25–30 amp breaker.

115V Plug-In units (9k and 12k models) run on a standard 115V / 60Hz circuit and only need a standard 20A household outlet — no new breaker or dedicated circuit required.

Always verify the exact requirements in the spec sheet for your specific unit. A licensed electrician can confirm your panel has the capacity.
Permit requirements vary by state, county, and city. Many jurisdictions require a mechanical permit for any HVAC installation, including mini splits. We recommend checking with your local building department before starting. If a permit is required, the inspection is usually straightforward and ensures the install is up to code.
Mini splits require minimal maintenance compared to traditional HVAC systems:

• Every 1–3 months: Clean or rinse the indoor unit’s washable air filters.
• Once a year: Clean the indoor coils and check the outdoor unit for debris, leaves, or ice buildup.
• As needed: Many units include a self-cleaning mode that cycles the coils to prevent mold and odor buildup.

Keeping filters clean is the single most impactful thing you can do to maintain efficiency and extend unit life.

115V vs. 230V — Plug-In Models

Understanding voltage options before you buy.

A 115V plug-in mini split runs on standard household current — the same voltage as your refrigerator or microwave. It plugs directly into a standard 20A, 115V outlet instead of requiring a dedicated 240V circuit. We carry the MRCOOL Advantage 5th Gen 115V in 9,000 and 12,000 BTU. They use the same pre-charged lineset installation as the standard units — just without the electrical work.
In most cases, no. If you already have a standard 115V/20A outlet near where the outdoor unit will go, no electrician is needed at all — the unit ships with a standard power cord that plugs right in. If you need to add a new 20A outlet, a licensed electrician can handle that quickly and inexpensively, far less involved than running a full 240V circuit.
Choose 115V if: you don’t have an existing 240V circuit nearby, you live in an apartment or condo, or you want the absolute simplest installation. Available in 9k and 12k BTU.

Choose 230V if: you already have a dedicated 240V circuit available, or you need an 18k, 24k, or 36k unit (those sizes are 230V only). The 230V versions are also slightly less expensive upfront. Both deliver the same comfort and efficiency once installed.
